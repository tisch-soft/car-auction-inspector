Why insurer-sold is the thing to filter on

An insurance company sells a given car exactly once — the day they total it. Everyone else selling on Copart is usually selling it again: bought cheap, patched up, photographed clean, and put back through at a markup. Copart publishes the seller on many lots, but gives you no way to search by it.

Questions people ask before they bid

Why does it matter who sold the car on Copart?

An insurance company sells a given car exactly once — the day they write it off. Anyone else selling on Copart is usually selling it again: bought cheap, repaired, photographed clean and put back through. The seller name is the single best clue to which of those you are looking at, and Copart shows it on many lots but gives you no way to search by it.

What does "minimum bid" mean on a Copart lot?

There is a hidden reserve. If the bidding does not reach it, the car does not sell and Copart puts it back on the calendar with a new date. Counted across every lot that ran in the last month, minimum-bid lots came back unsold about 56% of the time against 31% for pure-sale lots, so a pure sale is close to twice as likely to actually complete.

What does it mean when the same Copart lot keeps reappearing?

It went to auction and nobody met the seller’s price, so it was relisted — typically about four days later. Across the whole Copart calendar roughly 25% of lots that run come back rather than leave. A lot that has come back several times tells you roughly where the seller’s floor is, because you can see what the bidding reached and was refused.

Can a salvage car be exported from the United States?

US Customs requires the original certificate of title, or a certified copy, at least 72 hours before the vehicle leaves (19 CFR 192.2). A salvage title is a title, so it clears that test. A Certificate of Destruction, a Non-Repairable brand or a bill-of-sale-only lot is not a title and never becomes one, so those can only leave as parts. Whether your destination country will then register the car is a separate question with a different answer in every country.

Does Copart tell you whether a car has been sold at auction before?

No. Copart publishes what is for sale, not what happened to it — the public listing data carries no run count and no completed-sale flag. Third-party archives fill some of the gap but have no page at all for most live lots. The free Chrome extension on this site checks two archives and adds the runs it has watched itself.

How to check a Copart lot’s history without any tool

You can do all of this by hand, and it is worth knowing how:

  1. Get the VIN from the lot page. Signed out, Copart masks the last six characters — the VIN plate photo usually shows the rest.
  2. Search that VIN on a public auction archive. If the car appears with an earlier sale date and a different lot number, it has been through before.
  3. Compare the two sets of photographs. A rebuilt car photographs clean; the earlier listing is the only place the damage is still visible.
  4. Read the seller field. An insurer means a first loss. A dealer or a car-buying company means someone is selling it on.
  5. Read the title line in full. "Certificate of Destruction" and "Non-Repairable" can never be registered again in any state, whatever the photos look like.

The extension above does steps 1 to 5 on the page you are already looking at. This list does step 4 for the whole catalog, every night.
