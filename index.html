Why insurer-sold is the thing to filter on

An insurance company sells a given car exactly once — the day they total it. Everyone else selling on Copart is usually selling it again: bought cheap, patched up, photographed clean, and put back through at a markup. Copart publishes the seller on many lots, but gives you no way to search by it.

So this list does. Every night it reads Copart's own listing data for every lot with a sale date, keeps the ones an insurer consigned, and applies the filters above. Nothing here is scraped from a third party and nothing is guessed — seller, title, drive status and mileage are Copart's own fields, reproduced.

How to check a Copart lot's history without any tool

You can do all of this by hand, and it is worth knowing how:

  1. Get the VIN from the lot page. Signed out, Copart masks the last six characters — the VIN plate photo usually shows the rest.
  2. Search that VIN on a public auction archive. If the car appears with an earlier sale date and a different lot number, it has been through before.
  3. Compare the two sets of photographs. A rebuilt car photographs clean; the earlier listing is the only place the damage is still visible.
  4. Read the seller field. An insurer means a first loss. A dealer or a car-buying company means someone is selling it on.
  5. Read the title line in full. "Certificate of Destruction" and "Non-Repairable" can never be registered again in any state, whatever the photos look like.

The extension above does steps 1 to 5 on the page you are already looking at. This list does step 4 for the whole catalog, every night.
